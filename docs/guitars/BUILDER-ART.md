# Builder art: the seven Road guitars, in pieces the phone puts together

For Claude. Grok made this art on branch `rr-09b-art` for the guitar builder on the Merch table (design:
`docs/road-ready/images/r10-19-merch-builder.jpg`, finishes: r10-12, r10-13, r10-16, r10-17, r10-18). Nothing here is
generated by `tools/guitars/pics.py`; do not regenerate or hand edit these files. The paintings and `-road.webp`
files are unchanged.

## The idea in one paragraph

Each guitar keeps its painting (`art/guitars/<slug>-road.webp`) as the bottom layer. On top of it the app draws a
few layers cut from that same painting: where the paint is, where the guard is, where the metal is, and how the light
falls on each. A new colour or texture is drawn only inside those areas and then given the painting's own light and
shade back, so it sits in the picture. Every layer is the exact pixel size of that guitar's `-road.webp` and is drawn
at (0, 0), so nothing has to be lined up by hand (one exception: `burst.webp` is a quarter size, see below). The
result is one picture per build, the same size as `-road.webp`. Use it everywhere the guitar is shown: the builder's
big case picture (scaled to fit) and the Road neck (in place of `-road.webp`, same 1.2 times scale, so every fret,
string and stop position in `pics.js` still matches).

## Files

`art/guitars/builder/manifest.json` (12 KB) lists everything below, the colours of every finish, which options each
guitar has, and the fallbacks. Read it rather than hard coding.

Per guitar, in `art/guitars/builder/<slug>/` (all WebP, same width and height as `<slug>-road.webp`):

| File | What it is | How to draw it |
|---|---|---|
| `paint.webp` | Alpha: where new paint goes (body, plus the headstock face on the electrics). RGB: grey light and shade, 50% grey means no change. Strings, binding, soundholes, pickups, bridge, knobs and the fretboard are left out, so they stay painted. | Clip the paint to its alpha. Draw its RGB over the paint with `overlay`. |
| `top.webp` | Alpha: the face of the body for a flame or koa top. On electrics it stops 3 px short of the edge, which keeps a thin edge in the top's rim colour. | Clip the top texture to it. |
| `grain.webp` | Grey detail of the painting's wood or finish (50% grey means no change). Alpha matches `paint.webp`. | `overlay`, at the finish's `grain` opacity (0.05 for gloss black up to 0.85 for bursts). |
| `burst.webp` | Alpha only, **quarter size**: 1 at the body's outer edge, falling to 0 about a third of the way in. 0.35 on the headstock. | Draw scaled up to the picture's size with smoothing on. Edge colour through it once for a wide burst; mask it by itself (`destination-in`) for alpha squared, a narrow burst. |
| `light.webp` | White, alpha = the painting's bright gloss highlights on paint and guard. | `source-over` after the shade, at `light` opacity: finish light times wear light (new 1, played 0.6, road worn 0.3). |
| `guard.webp` | Only on guitars with a guard. Alpha: the guard. RGB: its own grey light and shade (screw holes and edge bevel included). On guitars with a painted guard the outer 1 px edge line is left out, so the painted bevel shows round every guard. | Clip the guard texture or colour to it, `overlay` its RGB, then the guard part of `light.webp`. |
| `metal.webp` | Alpha: tuner posts and shafts, bridge, tailpiece, knobs, switch and strap buttons; on Club Slab also the pole pieces and most guard screws. | For gold or aged nickel: inside it, take what is drawn, make it grey (`saturation` with a grey fill), then fill the metal colour with `overlay`. Chrome draws nothing (the painting is chrome). |
| `wear-played.webp` | RGBA: fine scratches, a dulled pick patch and a few tiny chips. | `source-over`, after the paint, before the guard. |
| `wear-worn.webp` | RGBA: bare wood at the forearm (bass side, top of the lower bout), pick wear, edge chips, with dark grime round them. The wood is already lit for this guitar. | Same. |
| `strings-long.webp` | RGBA: six string ends running out past the tuner buttons, each coiled once. Steel on the electrics, bronze on the acoustics. Stays inside the `-road.webp` frame, but reaches outside the guitar's outline, so the picture's alpha grows there. | `source-over`, last. |

Shared textures, `art/guitars/builder/tex/` (load only the one the player picks):

| File | Size (px) | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `flame.webp` | 512 x 768 | Grey flame maple figure with a bookmatch seam in the middle. `multiply` over the top's burst colours. |
| `koa.webp` | 512 x 768 | Figured koa, in colour. |
| `pearloid.webp`, `tortoise.webp`, `marble.webp`, `leopard.webp`, `tiger.webp`, `cow.webp`, `paisley.webp`, `sunflower.webp`, `checker.webp`, `chrome.webp` | 512 x 512 | Guards. Mint and Black are plain colours in the manifest. |
| `flake.webp` | 256 x 256 | White sparkles on clear, a repeating pattern from (0, 0), for metallic and flake paints. |

Textures are drawn at 1:1 picture pixels with their centre on the manifest's `texture_anchor` for that guitar (body
centre for tops, guard centre for guards). At that size they cover every body and guard, so they never repeat. Only
`flake.webp` repeats, and it tiles cleanly. Do not scale textures up: the leopard spots and flames are sized for it.

## Order and blend modes

Draw once per build into an offscreen canvas the size of `-road.webp`, keep the result (an ImageBitmap or a blob
URL keyed by the build), and only redraw when a choice changes.

1. **Base:** `-road.webp`.
2. **Paint** (skip if the paint is the painting's own and there is no new top). Into a scratch canvas:
   - Fill the colour (solid, metallic, flake), or the burst: centre colour, then `mid` (or `edge`) through `burst.webp`,
     then `edge` through burst squared. Metallic paints with an `edge` get one pass of it.
   - New top: inside `top.webp`, flame tops are the burst of the top's colours `multiply` `flame.webp`; koa is
     `koa.webp` with its `edge` colour through the burst at `edge_strength`. If the paint is the painting's own, the
     rest of `paint.webp` (the thin edge on electrics and the headstock face) takes the top's `rim` colour.
   - `grain.webp` with `overlay` at the finish's `grain` opacity (0.45 for a top over the painting's own paint).
   - `paint.webp` RGB with `overlay` (the light and shade).
   - Flake: `flake.webp` pattern, `source-over`, at the finish's `flake` opacity, stronger where the shade is light.
   - `light.webp`, `source-over`, at finish light times wear light.
   - Clip the scratch canvas to `paint.webp`'s alpha (`destination-in`) and draw it on the base.
3. **Wear:** `wear-played.webp` or `wear-worn.webp`, `source-over`.
4. **Guard** (only where the guitar has one, and only if the choice is not the painting's own): texture or colour,
   `overlay` the guard RGB, `light.webp` at the guard's `light` (mirror chrome 1.4) times wear light, clip to the
   guard alpha, draw. Road worn also multiplies the guard by `#f2dcae` at 0.45 (yellowed), the painted guard too.
5. **Metal** (gold or aged nickel, or any metal when road worn): grey, then `overlay` the colour, clip to
   `metal.webp`, draw. Aged nickel and road worn also darken it a little (see `dull` in the manifest).
6. **Strings left long:** `strings-long.webp`.

A sketch in canvas 2D (all of these modes are standard, and work in WKWebView):

```js
// ctx: the build canvas, w x h of -road.webp. img(name) returns a loaded image.
function clipped(fill, mask, op = 'source-over') { // fill then keep only inside mask
  const c = new OffscreenCanvas(w, h), x = c.getContext('2d');
  fill(x); x.globalCompositeOperation = 'destination-in'; x.drawImage(mask, 0, 0, w, h);
  ctx.globalCompositeOperation = op; ctx.drawImage(c, 0, 0); ctx.globalCompositeOperation = 'source-over';
}
ctx.drawImage(img('road'), 0, 0);
clipped(x => {
  x.fillStyle = paint.colour; x.fillRect(0, 0, w, h);          // or the burst passes
  x.globalAlpha = paint.grain; x.globalCompositeOperation = 'overlay'; x.drawImage(img('grain'), 0, 0);
  x.globalAlpha = 1; x.drawImage(img('paint'), 0, 0);           // light and shade, overlay
  x.globalCompositeOperation = 'source-over';
  x.globalAlpha = paint.light * wear.light; x.drawImage(img('light'), 0, 0); x.globalAlpha = 1;
}, img('paint'));
```

CSS alone can do it too (stacked images with `mix-blend-mode` and `mask-image`), but canvas is simpler and gives one
picture to reuse in the case and on the Road.

## Options per guitar

| Guitar | Paint | Headstock | Flame top | Guard | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Porch (acoustic) | all | stays painted wood | Koa only | added teardrop | The painting has no guard. Any guard choice draws an added teardrop (Cornerstone's shape, moved and scaled to Porch's soundhole). "Original" means no guard. |
| Cornerstone (acoustic) | all | stays painted wood | Koa only | painted teardrop | Light and shade are made, not taken from the painting (its burst can't be split from its light), so repaints look a little flatter. |
| Club Slab | all | takes the paint | all four | painted guard | |
| Hollow Hill | all | takes the paint | all four | none | The f-hole and pickups sit where a guard would go. |
| Swing | all | takes the paint (it was a darker slate) | all four | none | Carved top, no guard in the painting. |
| Koa Arc (acoustic) | all | stays painted wood | Koa only | none | Already koa: choosing Koa draws nothing new. Light and shade are made (the koa streaks are colour, not light). The seven ports sit where a guard would go. |
| Comet | all | takes the paint | all four | painted guard | Its glitter shows faintly through new paint (the grain layer); keep grain low for solid colours. |

Fallbacks (also in the manifest): flame maple tops (Teal, Violet, Purple) are for the electric bodies; on the
acoustics show those chips locked with a short line. Guard chips are hidden on Hollow Hill, Swing and Koa Arc; keep a
saved guard choice but don't draw it, so it comes back if the player moves to a guitar with a guard. Wear, metal and
long strings apply to all seven. On the acoustics the metal is the tuners only (bridge pins stay as painted).

## Check

`tools/guitars/builder_preview.py` puts a build together from these files in Python, following the steps above. Run
from the repo root: `python3 -I tools/guitars/builder_preview.py clubslab '{"paint":"seafoam","guard":"leopard"}' out.png`.
`docs/guitars/builder/builder-sheet.jpg` was made with it: Porch, Club Slab and Comet, each as painted and in two builds.

- Default build (every choice the painting's own) against `-road.webp`, all seven guitars: mean pixel error 0.0,
  largest 0.0 (out of 255). In a paint-only build, nothing outside `paint.webp`'s area changes (largest change 0.0, all seven).
- Every layer is the size of its `-road.webp`, and no layer draws outside the guitar's cut-out (the long strings are
  the one intended exception).
- Repainting each body in its own average colour, as a solid, with full grain and no highlights, against the
  painting, inside the paint area (mean error out of 255): Club Slab 1.8, Porch 3.6, Hollow Hill 4.7, Swing 5.2,
  Koa Arc 10.9, Cornerstone 11.7, Comet 12.2. The last three are higher because their paint is not one colour
  (koa streaks, a burst, glitter and big highlights), not because anything is out of line.

## What can't be done, or isn't done

- Only the seven Road guitars have builder art, not the 16 concepts.
- Headstocks keep their shape. The acoustic headstocks stay painted wood; electric headstocks take the paint (a
  burst gives them its middle tone, a top gives them its rim colour). There is no back or sides: the art is front on.
- Pickups (cream or black covers), tuner buttons (cream), the bridge saddles' shapes, soundholes, rosettes,
  binding, fretboards, inlays and strings stay as painted. Only their metal changes colour.
- Wear is the same pattern every time for a guitar (one per level), not random per player.
- Mirror chrome is a made reflection, it does not reflect the case.
- Cornerstone and Koa Arc repaints have made light and shade (see above), so they look a touch flatter than the
  other five.
- On Club Slab a few guard screws are under a print guard rather than cut out; the guard's shade still shows them.
- The design picture (r10-19) shows a different body in the case; this art is for our seven paintings.
- No logos, brand names or text in any file.

## Sizes

All new files, in bytes. Total for `art/guitars/builder/`: 1,164,981 bytes (about 1.1 MB). A guitar needs about
75 to 128 KB of its own files; textures are 3 to 74 KB each and load only when picked. Nothing here needs to be in
`sw.js` `SHELL` up front; load a guitar's files when the builder opens on it.
