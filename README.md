# Louie: Learn Guitar

A guitar app for the phone that plays like a game: the guitar is how you play. Learn chords on a path, play your songs as scored shows, keep a streak, earn fans, climb a weekly chart and build a band. Chord sheets and tabs, offline, everything on the device, no account.

Live at `https://grahamdeno.github.io/tabs/` (GitHub Pages from `main`). Add it to the home screen to use it as an app (it shows as Louie). It was called The Road from version 3 until 4.89.0, and Tabs before that; the repo and saved data keep the name tabs. The home tab and its path of stops are still called the Road.

## What it does

### The Road (home)
- **Road Ready (Light and Dark looks):** one bare row at the top with three numbers, each a small painting (5.6.0; a flame for the streak that turns to a charcoal ember when the streak is out, Map hexes for fans, a merch tee for tips): your gig streak (tap for a small card with the last seven days), your fans (tap for the Map) and your tips (tap for the Merch table). The Road is one rosewood fretboard you scroll up and down, centred, with a gutter on each side and a bone nut at the top. Frets follow real spacing and get closer together down the neck. Each stop is a pearl inlay between the wires: done ones are lit cool teal with a check, the current one glows, and locked ones are ivory with a padlock. The leg in view is one line under the stats (Opening night · First steps, with Skip on the right). The left gutter holds only the stop numbers. The current stop's name sits on a cream plate above the tab bar, with Start on the right. A leg change is a thick gold fret, and the next leg is drawn in full below it, grayed out, with its stops locked. Louie is not on the Road, and there is no Tonight bar there. Sheets opened from the Road still show him. At large text sizes the strip stacks; at the largest, the pips step aside. Reduce Motion stops the glow and the line drawing on. No tuner and no Rehearse button on the Road: the tuner is on the song screen and on the Tuner row on Me, and Rehearse is a tab. Green Room and Headliner keep the stat bar, leg banner and finger dots below.
- **The Tour tab is the Map (4.106.0):** the Tour tab opens straight onto the Map, with the tab bar along the bottom; the button at its top left opens this month's tour.
- A stat bar on every main page: your venue (tap for the Map), your gig streak, fans to spend (tap for the Merch table). The streak (tap for Your profile) is a guitar that rocks once you've played tonight and sits grey and upright until you do; tapping it rocks it, taps the phone and opens your profile.
- The leg you're on is a guitar neck (4.82.0), nut at the top and progress going down: each lesson is a finger dot on a string just behind a fret, a merch box is a single fret inlay, and the headline show is the double inlay at the end, then the guitar's body. Every leg has its own guitar (The beater, The cutaway, The solid-body and more), earned by headlining the leg before; a sheet says so once. The first guitar, Porch, is yours from the first launch (5.5.0): it is on the stand on Me as "Your first guitar" and the builder takes it before any show; headlining Tour 1 earns Cornerstone. Tap a stop for its card (what you'd earn, then Start; for a headline, the last song you played that fits and Other songs). Opening night keeps its picks.
- **Songs move you along too (4.83.0):** a chord's stop is done by its lesson or by playing 5 songs with that chord to the end. Its card says how many more songs it needs ("Or play E in 3 more songs, start to finish (2 of 5)") and offers the song with it you played last; a locked chord's card says songs count already. Show complete says what the song did on the Road ("The Road: E chord, 3 of 5 songs", or "E chord done").
- **Your guitarist (painted only, 2026-11-23):** wherever the app means you (your profile card and share card, your chart row, a rival battle, a duel, the Friends board) it shows one of 24 finished paintings: a man or a woman in each of 12 genres (rock, punk, metal, blues, country, folk, jazz, classical, indie, funk, reggae, pop), mirrored if you play left-handed. That is the whole choice: genre, man or woman, left-handed, and a dice button that picks one at random. The old character builder (skin, hair, beard, face, glasses, tops, patches, starters), Start from a selfie and Make my portrait are gone; a saved guitarist keeps only its genre, who and left-handed on load. The mood follows the night (sleepy when tonight is the last chance to keep a streak, cheering on a milestone, worried in a duel you lost) and the stage card behind a painting follows the venue you've reached. Computer players on the chart get a painting chosen from their name. Louie stays the mascot everywhere else (the end of a gig, tips, Opening night). Made in Opening night's first step, Who's playing tonight? (pick a genre and man or woman, THAT'S ME; no fans), or once on the Road for a player past it (Skip keeps the default); changed any time with Edit your guitarist on Your profile (`guitarist.js`, `maker.js`).
- **Lean Road (4.99.0):** a smaller header strip, no floating Songbook bar in Light and Dark, the next leg shown locked under the one you're on, Opening night on its own guitar with Tour 1 locked below it, and engraved plates instead of comic bursts for what a show paid.
- **Guitars and inlays (4.83.1, 4.84.0):** each leg's guitar has its own body with a nod to a classic (a parlor, a dreadnought cutaway, an offset double cut on a maple neck with six tuners in line, a twin-horn hollow body, a single cut, a classical, our own headliner) and its own inlay material (pearl, black, mother of pearl, abalone, gold). The stop decides the shape: a merch box is a crate on every guitar, the headline show two of the guitar's own inlays. A lesson whose chord would open songs you have carries a small badge with how many ("♪ 2").
- **The merch box is a fret inlay you open (4.82.0):** once you reach it, it's your next stop: an empty pocket with a dashed gold ring and a "Tap to open" tag. A tap sets the pearl in, with a POW for its 25 fans. Boxes reached before 4.76.0 count as opened.
- **The next stop says its name (4.75.0):** a headline show is the double inlay, so shows stand apart from lessons. The next stop carries a small tag with its name; only that one.
- **The Nano guitars (4.86.0):** each leg of the Road is drawn on its own guitar (Parlor, Cutaway, Solid, Hollow, Single, Classical, Headliner), with the neck fat on purpose so stops are easy to see and the strings fanning to the bridge. A plain neck shows for a moment while the art loads. The new-guitar sheet shows the whole guitar. Louie was redrawn (rounded handle, chrome corners, knobs on the amp face, slimmer mitten hands); the guitarist has a new head with ear lines, 13 hair styles (a long mohawk is new) and the same mitten hands in skin tone.  4.87.0: the guitars are Gemini's pictures, used as pictures: a real headstock, a narrow neck like a real one (the stops sit on it, one fret per stop) and the body below, wider than the screen. The guitarist holds a small copy of the guitar you're on. Pictures are in `art/guitars/` (`tools/guitars/pics.py` cuts them out and measures them); the guitarist's head and hair are in `art/guitarist.js` (`tools/guitarist`).
- **Build your guitar (Road Ready 9b):** a section on the Merch table opens the builder for the Road guitars you've earned: the guitar in a case at the top, a switch between your guitars, chips for Body, Flame top, Guard, Wear and Hardware, rows of 44 px swatches, Leave strings long, and Save to case with the running total in tips. Stock parts are free; shop parts open at Coffeehouse ($25 to $45), custom parts and print guards at Club, flame and koa tops at Theater ($120). A part is bought once and is free on every guitar; a locked swatch shows a lock and says "Locked, opens at Club". Acoustics take koa but not the flame tops; Hollow Hill, Swing and Koa Arc have no guard. The built guitar shows on the stand in the case on Me. It does not replace the Road, which stays the rosewood neck. It is put together once on the phone (`buildart.js`) from Grok's layers in `art/guitars/builder/`; a guitar left as painted shows its painting. Prices are `BUILD_PARTS` in `core/game.js`; saves are `data.builds` and `b-<id>` in `merch.has`. Looks only.
- **Road Ready guitars (9a):** each leg of the Road is a new guitar with a rounded 3+3 headstock and painted strings: Porch (Tour 1), Cornerstone, Club Slab, Hollow Hill, Swing, Koa Arc and Comet (the Encore guitar). Earning is as before: headlining a leg earns the next leg's guitar, and saves don't change. The older pictures are still built but no leg uses them. **The Guitar shop** is the last section on the Merch table: 16 more guitars (6 punk, 10 custom) that open by venue and cost tips, from Open mic ($90) to Stadium ($300). A locked guitar says "Opens at Club", an open one you can't yet afford says "$40 more", and one is tagged Next up. Prices and venues are one constant (`GUITAR_SHOP` in `core/game.js`). A bought guitar can be put on: it shows on the stand in the case on Me, on Show complete and in your guitarist's hands. It does not replace the rosewood neck. It is looks only: fans, tips earned, scores and what a leg earns never change. **The Road is the rosewood neck**, not these paintings. A tap on a stop opens its card. Pictures: `docs/guitars/` (Grok's art and the manifest), built by `tools/guitars/pics.py` into `art/guitars/`.
- **Opening night (4.40.0):** a new player starts on a short leg of the rosewood neck before Tour 1 that teaches by doing, on the sample song: Who's playing tonight? (4.85.0: make your guitarist, no fans), Hold the guitar, The open strings, Let me hear you (Louie says why the app listens, then the phone asks for the microphone; Not now plays unscored), Your first notes, Tap a chord (a strip above Play says which), Play a verse (the Gig waits for Count me in and says to strum on each change), Pause and pick up (tap, then Resume) and Your first show. A show played through finishes the steps it skipped. +5 fans a step. Skip on that line asks first; Settings > Help > Tips & gestures replays it. Anyone who had a play or a lesson before it never sees it. Afterwards the stat bar names Venue, Streak and Fans with a › until each is tapped once.
- One line for the moment and tonight's song to play, in a plain card beside the path (Louie is not on the Road).
- The Songbook bar above the tab bar: your songs, how many you can play, and search.
- Fans: 10 for every play, 5 for every lesson round, 25 for every merch box you open.
- The tab bar: five amp knobs, Road, Songbook, Rehearse, Tour and Me, on a dark grille bar with a chrome edge (a cream grille bar in Light, on every screen). The chosen knob is lit cool teal and its label turns teal; the others are dark knobs with cream labels (ink labels on the cream bar). Me is Your profile. In Dark and Light it shows your name and level, one slim row of songs, chords and plays, your guitars on a stand, the Merch table, then Milestones, Your band and Charts and duels in one card, plus the Tuner and Support Louie, and a Settings gear at the top right. Those pages, and `#/settings`, keep their own addresses and a back button that returns to Me. A pink dot on the Me tab and the Tuner row says you haven't tuned lately. From large text sizes (AX3) the labels hide and the knobs grow; each tab keeps its name for a screen reader.

### Playing a song
- The song page (4.31.0): beside the title, the song's cover (Apple Music art, or its letter on a colour) and "about this song" under the artist; both open About this song. Under the title, Mark as learning; each tap moves it on through the three mastery levels, Learning, Solid and Nailed it, then clears it (Road Ready 07). At the bottom just Tools and Play, and Play's second line says what it plays with. About this song: the cover with a preview, Tempo, Length and Key tiles (Key opens the key dial), a word when the recording doesn't match the sheet with Match it, When you press Play (With the recording or With the band, saved per song), Timing and Change recording, and Done at the bottom. With no recording linked it offers Find a recording.
- Anywhere in a song, a short scroll up shows a slim bar with Back and the title (4.42.2); tap the title to go back to the top.
- The text size fits the song (4.51.0): the largest size, from 14 to 22 px, that puts the song's longest line on one line, so a line of the song is a line on screen; Text size in Tools scales it up or down. A row of chords with no words (an intro, a solo) spreads across the width and is timed evenly along it.
- Play on any song is one page (4.51.0 to 4.53.0): the song page is the stage. Played as a show, "the Gig" (auto-scroll, the recording or the band); otherwise (practice, a tab, Play together) it is the same page with nothing on top. Since 4.91.0 the show doesn't listen: catching chords in a moving song was never reliable enough to score on (`docs/NO-CATCH-PLAN.md`). The microphone is for the tuner, lessons and the Listening booth.
- While it plays the title, chips, Tools and Play step back and the song fills the screen:
  - **The line you are on** sits on a band in the section's colour that runs edge to edge (4.49.14), so time after the words reads as space. The next line is nearly full, lines ahead stay readable, lines behind fade. Every chord takes the section's colour, and a quiet stage light in that colour sits behind the words and fades out above the band.
  - **Played through** (4.91.0): a chord change counts when the song carries it past the reading line while playing (not dragged past, not paused); the phone can't tell whether you strummed, so it's the honour system a play already runs on. A chord repeated in a row is one change, shown once. No beat dots, no lit chord, no green or missed marks. A chord you have not learned gets a small shape card, low on the right (NOW for the one you are on, else NEXT).
  - **Quick changes and notes on a chord line (4.83.0):** "C-G" is two chords, each shown on its own; a note in brackets ("(hold)", "(let ring)") is shown small and is never a chord.
  - **The words never run under the top (4.83.0):** the band behind the section name and the crowd is solid past the crowd, then fades, so a line scrolling up can't show through them, over a song's cover too (blurred dark glass).
  - **The sections:** the page's own rail went in 5.3.1 (rarely used), and its room went to the lines. To move through a song, scroll it, tap a line to start from there, or use the right pedal for the next section. There is no bar over the stage.
  - **In a show only:** the section and the run ("14 in a row", changes since the last pause, with a check) at the top, and a crowd of 12 fans on its own row that gets on its feet as the song goes (those up stand and throw their hands up). A run of 10, 25, 50 or 100 bursts IN A ROW over the show. The stage light is one cone from the lamp down in the section's colour.
  - **Reading and moving:** the text size fits the song (the largest of 14 to 22 px that keeps its longest line on one line; Text size in Tools scales it). Drag the song itself to move it. A row of chords with no words spreads across the width and is timed evenly. No Lines setting, no Full, no timing bar.
  - **The band:** the Metronome switch in Tools is the band's switch in a show (4.49.12): off silences Beat too, and it reads on whenever drums play. After a stall the band skips the beats that passed on the same grid, and the count-in is timed from its first beat.
- **Song stars** (4.91.0): each song climbs four stars: Learning (in the Songbook), Rehearsed (played through 3 times), Gig-ready (the whole song at full speed without pausing; 25 fans) and Headliner (Gig-ready on 3 different days; 50 fans). Full speed means no Practice mode slow-down and no Build up. The song page shows them beside the artist, and Show complete celebrates a new one. A song scored 80% or more before 4.91.0 starts Gig-ready. Wait for me and Follow my tempo (4.54.0, 4.80.0) went with the listening in 4.91.0.
- **Readability (4.89.0):** nothing smaller than 12 px; nothing that floats over a song (sheets, toasts, callouts, the Gig's NOW card) is see-through, even on album art; locked lessons show a lock instead of fading; disabled buttons keep their words; text wraps instead of being cut off; toasts rise full width from the bottom, next to their Undo. A browser test (`tests/e2e/readable.e2e.mjs`) checks size and contrast on the main screens. `docs/READABILITY-PLAN.md` has the rules.
- **Louie's count-in** (4.56.0, was the opening card of 4.55.0): with a count-in on, the empty space above the first line holds Louie and a big number stepping 1 to 4, Louie bouncing on each beat and throwing horns up on the last, with one small line under it: the chord you start on and the strum ("First: F · Strum: D-DU-UDU"). It fades as soon as the song moves. With count-in off, or starting mid-song, there is no card. With less room Louie and the number shrink; with reduced motion he stays still and only the number changes.

- **Song tours (5.9.0, rounds 5.10.0):** after each finished skill tour that has chord steps (Open chords, Chord switches) the Tour gets a Song tour: one real song you would probably like, learned one chord at a time. Rounds start at 60% speed the first time and at 100% once played through (60, 80 or 100 any time). At 100% with a recording lined up (Apple Music or YouTube) the recording plays that part and the blocks follow it; otherwise Louie's band plays at the song's tempo. Louie listens during a round only: a strum from half a beat before to one beat after a marked strum is checked for the chord, and a round passes when he hears at least 3 in 4. The card says "Louie heard 7 of 9", with Next and Play it again (no penalty); with the mic off a round counts when played through and the card says Louie wasn't listening. Timing is never graded. VoiceOver hears the chord and what is next once per bar. The 5.9.0 stand-in chords are gone. A song tour never holds the next skill tour back. ### Tuner
- Acoustic and electric headstocks in colour (acoustic is wood; electric matches Louie's finish) with each key labelled, the note big at the top with what to do beside it, a bolder meter that eases into the last few cents, the instrument and tuning in one menu at the top, Guided (low to high) and Chromatic with adjustable A4. Standard, Drop D, half and whole step down, DADGAD, Open G and Open D.
- The tuner opens from Rehearse and every lesson (4.24.0). Each string you tune ticks (Android vibrates; iPhone gets the system tick from iOS 18) and chimes, reaching the in-tune zone gives a light tick, and the whole guitar gets three ticks, a louder chime and a small party with Louie. The chimes are loud enough to hear over the guitar, each string a little louder than the last, and the finish rings out as a chord (4.42.7). In a lesson, Start listening turns into "Listening · tap to stop" and comes back by itself if the microphone stops.

## Chord sheet format

```
[Verse]
G              C        G
Blackbird singing in the dead of night

[Chorus]
[C]Take these [G]broken wings

[Verse 2]
Em            C
  Down the river   slowly
```

A chord over the indent before the first word (two spaces or more) is played before the singing starts (4.49.6): it stands ahead of the words with a short rest mark under it, and in time with the recording it comes a bar before the first word. A chord over a gap between words still lands on the next word.

### About the API key

The key is stored in the browser's local storage and sent straight from the phone to `api.anthropic.com`. That's a reasonable trade for a personal app with no server, with limits:

- Anyone with the unlocked phone can read it in Settings.
- Any script on the page could read it. The app loads no third-party scripts, escapes all user text, and its content security policy only lets it talk to `api.anthropic.com`, Apple's song search, LRCLIB, GetSongBPM and Open-Meteo.

Use a key made only for Tabs with a low monthly spend limit, and revoke it in the Console if the phone is lost. Keys are never put in backups.

Conventions:
- Build markup with html`` from `core.js`, which escapes every value unless it's safe markup (another html``, an icon, or `raw()`). Older templates use `esc()` on every value. A browser test fills every name with markup and checks none of it runs.
- Shared state lives in the module that owns it; other modules read it through imports and change it only through that module's functions (`setData`, `setOpenEdit`, `setSetCtx`, `setFilter`). ESLint's `no-import-assign` catches a direct write.
- The app never uses the phone's alert boxes; `ask()` shows a short sheet instead. Discarding something happens at once with Undo rather than a question.
- Accessibility: every control has a VoiceOver name, panels are labelled dialogs that take focus and close with Escape, focus is always outlined, text meets contrast in the light and dark palettes (axe runs in CI), and Reduce Motion turns animation off.
- Wide screens: from 700 px text is larger; from 1000 px the song gets a rail with the chord chips; from 1150 px a song list on the left. A phone on its side gets a slim column of controls.

Data: the library lives in IndexedDB with a copy in localStorage written on every save; at start the newer readable one wins. Older saves migrate automatically. [docs/DATA.md](docs/DATA.md) describes every field, the backup file and how a restore is checked.

Problems: the last 20 errors are kept on the phone (4.42.8: problems from an older version are listed under "Before" and drop off after 30 days), and the first in a session shows a note with Copy details. Settings > Help > Copy diagnostics copies the version, device, counts and storage, never song words, titles or keys.

### Releases

`VERSION` in `core.js` and `CACHE` in `sw.js` must match (a test checks). A feature is a minor release (2.30.0); a fix is a patch on the release where the defect was found (2.30.1). Nothing bumps the version automatically: Sonnet bumps `VERSION` and `CACHE` together on `next`, in one commit, before Graham tests a release. The app opens from its saved copy, so a change only reaches installed apps through a new cache name; on the next launch it downloads the release in the background and offers to reload. Parts loaded later are requested as `tuner.js?v=1.2.3` so they always come from the same release as the open app.

### Tests

- `node --test` runs the unit tests (no dependencies): chord parsing, transposing, wrapping, fingerings, library data and backups, pitch detection on synthetic and real recordings (`tests/audio/`), tip pacing, escaping, and the version matching the cache.
- Browser tests run the real app in Chromium: `npm i --no-save playwright axe-core && npx playwright install chromium`, then `node --test --test-concurrency=4 tests/e2e/*.e2e.mjs`. `app`, `app2` and `app3` cover features end to end (with Play set to plain scrolling); `gig` covers the Gig, the main Play path; `regress` walks the main path at 320, 390 and 1180 wide in both themes; `a11y` checks names and axe findings; `visual` compares screens against `tests/visual/ci/`.
- `node tests/quick.mjs` runs the unit tests plus only the browser tests that cover files changed since `origin/main`.
- GitHub Actions runs everything plus ESLint on every push. Visual references are remade with the update screenshots workflow after an intended change to how screens look.
